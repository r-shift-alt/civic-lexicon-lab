## User-Job Note

This entry is meant to help a bilingual political writer, editor, or translator decide whether a German use of `soziale Marktwirtschaft` should be translated literally as `social market economy`, kept in German, or translated with a short gloss that restores the postwar German institutional and historical baggage.

The likely user error is to assume that `social market economy` works in English as a transparent generic label for capitalism with welfare protections, when German `soziale Marktwirtschaft` often functions as a founding economic-order formula tied to ordoliberal principles, postwar West German development, and mainstream party self-description.

## Provisional Mismatch Hypothesis

`soziale Marktwirtschaft` and `social market economy` overlap at the level of naming a market order that rejects both laissez-faire and command economy, but the German term more often carries specific postwar, ordoliberal, and institutional baggage while the English phrase often functions as explanatory shorthand unless the surrounding sentence restores that baggage explicitly.

## Drafting Claim Check

### What seems genuinely shared
Both `soziale Marktwirtschaft` and `social market economy` can name a market order that rejects both pure laissez-faire and command economy and that combines competition with some commitment to social protection or social balance.

### What seems mismatched
German `soziale Marktwirtschaft` more often works as a historically loaded formula for the postwar Federal Republic's economic order, with ordoliberal and institutional baggage attached. English `social market economy` is usually the right literal rendering, but it more often reads as a translated label or explanatory shorthand unless the sentence restores the German historical and institutional context.

### What advice a writer or translator may need
Translate the term literally when the source is naming the concept, official model, or party-state self-description. Add a short gloss when the German source is relying on the term's historical and institutional resonance rather than spelling it out. In English, phrases like `Germany's postwar social market economy model` or `the German social market economy, combining competition with a social-policy framework` will often preserve more meaning than the bare phrase alone.

## Drafting Warning to Carry Forward

Do not write the entry as if `social market economy` were wrong.
The problem is narrower: the literal rendering is usually necessary, but it often under-signals that `soziale Marktwirtschaft` is a specific German economic-order formula with postwar lineage, not just a generic way to say `moderated capitalism`.

## Candidate Reusable Warning Sentence

If the German source invokes `soziale Marktwirtschaft` as a founding economic-order formula rather than a loose description of capitalism with welfare provisions, translate it literally but add a short gloss; otherwise English readers may miss the postwar German and ordoliberal baggage doing the real work.
