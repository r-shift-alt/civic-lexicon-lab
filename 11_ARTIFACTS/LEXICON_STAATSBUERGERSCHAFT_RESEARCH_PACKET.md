## User-Job Note

This entry is meant to help a bilingual political writer, editor, or translator decide whether a German use of `Staatsbürgerschaft` should be rendered directly as `citizenship`, and when a nearby German discussion is really about civic-mindedness, public spirit, or `Bürgersinn` rather than legal membership alone.

The likely user error is to assume that every English use of `citizenship` maps back cleanly onto German `Staatsbürgerschaft`, even when the English context is really about democratic participation, civic education, or responsible public conduct.

## Provisional Mismatch Hypothesis

`Staatsbürgerschaft` and `citizenship` overlap strongly when the topic is formal membership, naturalization, or the rights-and-duties relationship between person and state, but English `citizenship` more readily expands into civic participation, democratic formation, and responsible public behavior, where German may need `Bürgersinn`, `staatsbürgerliche Verantwortung`, or another civic-language gloss instead of plain `Staatsbürgerschaft`.

## Drafting Claim Check

### What seems genuinely shared
Both `Staatsbürgerschaft` and `citizenship` can name a formal relationship of membership between a person and a state, especially in contexts involving naturalization, eligibility, rights, duties, and status.

### What seems mismatched
German `Staatsbürgerschaft` appears more tightly anchored to legal membership and the state-person relationship, while English `citizenship` can also operate as a broader label for democratic participation, civic education, belonging, or responsible public conduct.

### What advice a writer or translator may need
Use `citizenship` directly when the German source is clearly about legal status, nationality law, naturalization, or the rights-and-duties bundle attached to formal membership. Add a boundary note, gloss, or different civic term when the relevant meaning is really about public-mindedness, civic ethos, or active democratic participation rather than legal membership itself.

## Drafting Warning to Carry Forward

Do not write the entry as if `citizenship` were wrong.
The problem is narrower: `citizenship` is often the clean rendering for `Staatsbürgerschaft`, but English also uses `citizenship` for civic-formation and participation language that German may express through `Bürgersinn` or another civic term instead.

## Candidate Reusable Warning Sentence

If the German source is about legal membership in the state, `citizenship` is usually the right rendering; if the surrounding argument is really about civic-mindedness, public spirit, or democratic participation, do not assume `Staatsbürgerschaft` is still the best German match just because English keeps using `citizenship`.
